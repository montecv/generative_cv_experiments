# generative_cv_experiments

A personal collection of from-scratch implementations and experiments with
**generative models for computer vision**. Each subproject is self-contained:
a small PyTorch module plus a notebook that trains it and visualizes the results.

This repo grows over time as I explore more architectures and ideas.

## Contents

### VAE — `vae.py`, `vae_experiments.ipynb`
A Variational Autoencoder. Includes a convolutional variant and a minimal linear
variant, the reparametrization trick, a KL term against a standard normal prior,
and notebooks that show reconstructions and the structure of the latent space
(sampling from `N(0, I)` vs. the encoder's posterior).

### VQ-VAE — `vq_vae.py`, `vq_vae_experiments.ipynb`
A Vector-Quantized VAE with a discrete codebook. Several design choices live behind
one shared interface and toggle with flags:
- **`use_ema`** — gradient-trained codebook (two-term VQ loss) vs. an EMA-updated
  codebook that is more robust against codebook collapse.
- **`normalize`** — L2-normalize encoder outputs and codebook before the
  nearest-neighbour search, turning it into a cosine match. This curbs codebook-norm
  drift and markedly improves codebook usage.

The encoder/decoder use bilinear (resize-conv) upsampling for sharp reconstructions. 
The notebook covers codebook-from-data initialization, straight-through gradient 
estimation, reconstructions, and a codebook-usage diagnostic that shows how many codes are 
actually active (a direct way to spot collapse), comparing the variants side by side.

### Transformer prior — `transformer_prior.py` (lives in the VQ-VAE notebook)
A compact, decoder-only transformer trained as an autoregressive 
**prior over the VQ-VAE's discrete codes**. Once the VQ-VAE has a trained codebook, every
image becomes a sequence of code indices; the transformer learns their distribution, so a
brand-new image can be generated by sampling a fresh code sequence and decoding it. This is
what turns the autoencoder into a real generative model — unlike a VAE, where sampling from
`N(0, I)` mostly yields noise, a learned prior produces coherent images.

The notebook walks through token extraction, transformer training (with warmup + cosine LR
schedule and gradient clipping), and sampling with temperature / top-k / top-p control.

### dVAE — `dvae.py`, `dvae_experiments.ipynb`
A discrete VAE that replaces hard nearest-neighbour quantization with the
**Gumbel-Softmax** relaxation. The encoder emits logits over the codebook entries;
adding Gumbel noise and taking a temperature-scaled softmax yields a differentiable
soft selection over codes, so gradients reach the encoder directly — no
straight-through trick needed. The codebook is learned jointly.

Temperature is annealed from high (soft, near-uniform mixing) to low (peaky,
near one-hot) over training, with evaluation done at low temperature for
near-discrete codes. 

## Dataset

Experiments are trained on **LFW (Labeled Faces in the Wild)**, center-cropped to
the face and resized to 64×64. The dataset is **not** included in this repo —
download it separately and point `PATH_TO_PHOTOS` in the notebooks at your local
copy
